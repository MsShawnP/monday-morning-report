# What Cinderhaven's Founder Sees Every Monday Morning

## 12 weeks of Monday reports at $25M, with commentary

---

Cinderhaven is a $25M specialty food brand distributed across natural, conventional grocery, and club channels. The founder — who still runs the Monday morning check herself — adopted a three-number pulse in late Q3. By week 12, she had caught one significant problem early and prevented a shelf-position loss that would have cost an estimated $80,000–$150,000 in annual velocity.

The three numbers she tracks (read against the template's top tier, $15M–$20M — Cinderhaven, at $25M, sits just above the template's stated $3M–$20M range and uses the top-tier metric set):

| # | Metric | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Revenue vs plan by channel (MTD) | Catches channel mix drift before month close |
| 2 | Cash conversion status (owed/disputed/expected) | Deductions don't show in AR aging until it's too late |
| 3 | Growth pipeline — new retailer timeline + capacity | A slipped first-ship date almost always signals a deeper problem |

## The Week 6 Story

On October 7, the Monday pulse showed Walmart velocity on Cinderhaven's top SKU down 15% week-over-week: 4.8 to 4.1 units per store per week. Nothing else explained it — revenue was fine, cash was clean, the growth pipeline was back on track after the slotting fee delay in week 5.

The founder called the Walmart category manager that morning and asked for a planogram scan at the underperforming stores. The answer came back in week 8: a recent reset had shifted the SKU from eye level to bottom shelf at roughly a third of the division's stores.

She submitted a reset request the same day and scheduled a store-check visit.

By week 9, velocity had started to recover. By week 12, it was back to baseline.

---

## What This Cost, and What It Would Have Cost

The reset process took two weeks of lead time and one field visit. Total cost: a few hundred dollars in travel and roughly four hours of the founder's time.

The quarterly category review would have surfaced this same problem — in early January, ten weeks later. By that point, Walmart's buyer would have seen the velocity decline in their own scorecard and likely initiated a conversation about the SKU's placement. At that stage, defending shelf position requires promotional spend and margin concession.

A lost eye-level shelf position at a major retailer on a top-performing SKU is worth $80,000–$150,000 in annual velocity, depending on the division size. The Monday pulse didn't prevent the planogram shift. It made the shift visible early enough to fix it before it became a decision Cinderhaven didn't get to make.

## A Note on the Numbers

Cinderhaven's Monday report is not complicated. Three numbers. One action note per metric. Two minutes to fill in. After 12 weeks, the founder has a trend view she has never had before — not because the data didn't exist, but because it was never assembled in one place at a consistent weekly cadence.

The discipline is the product. The spreadsheet is just the container.
